How it works

  1. Collect119 numbers from 20 public repositories, each with its exact sentence and the commit that wrote it.
  2. DownloadRaw data from DefiLlama, NearBlocks and a NEAR archival node, saved byte for byte with SHA-256 hashes.
  3. RecomputeEvery number for the day it was published, under each standard definition (30, 90 or 365 days…).
  4. Build and testA thesis from what replicates. Its 5 kill conditions run as code and are re-checked every day.

Re-checked every day. Newest snapshot 2026-11-12 (data to 2026-11-11, 2 snapshots so far): all 5 kill conditions hold; 90-day on-chain cover of net issuance 18.3%; past days changed by DefiLlama since the snapshot before: 0. Every snapshot side by side.

Three findings

What comparing 20 teams shows that no single thesis can

Confirmed independently

1The teams agree because they read one source

All 15 teams that publish NEAR Intents volume, fees or revenue cite DefiLlama, which reads datasets NEAR itself uploads to Dune. Their agreement is one measurement read 15 times, not 15 confirmations.

So we measured revenue a second way. It arrives as NEAR in three public wallets, and an archival node can read their balances at past blocks. Their growth is 0.98–1.01× the reported revenue over 30, 90 and 360 days.

The revenue figure behind every Intents thesis is real, and it can be checked on chain without trusting anyone.

Explained

2Where the numbers differ, the definition differs, not the data

Three teams publish NEAR's price change over a year: +19%, +96% and +97%. All three are right. Comparing two single days gives +19%; comparing two 30-day averages gives +98%, because the price jumped in early November 2025.

The same holds for base-layer fees in dollars (-55%, -40% or -28% in a year): each number is reproduced within 5% once the window and the end day match.

A single number without its definition means little. The spread between teams is the honest error bar.

Unexplained

3One figure does not replicate, and we say so

3 teams print 30-day Intents fees of $5,458,382 (or a rounded version), all committed the same day. Our total for the day before is $5.96M, 9% higher. No 30-day window in the chart comes within 5%: the closest is $5.78M, for the window ending 8 Nov.

Meanwhile 2 other teams' fee totals land exactly on our line on their own publication days. Nor did the source rewrite its history: two snapshots a day apart agree on every past day.

We publish what we cannot explain instead of tuning it away.

The investment thesis

NEAR is a growth bet on Intents revenue, not a bargain

NEAR creates about 2.5% new tokens a year. Revenue from NEAR Intents now offsets part of that dilution, and the base layer offsets almost none. The thesis holds as long as that revenue keeps growing and stays in the wallets.

Evidence for

  • Demand moved to Intents and grows: $32.9B settled in a year; the 30-day average is +58% on a year ago.
  • NEAR keeps a rising share of fees: 17% over a year, 30% over the last 30 days.
  • The revenue is verifiable: the wallets hold 4.24M NEAR and their total has never fallen.
  • The offset is rising: 11.2% of new supply in the first half of the year, 14.5% in the second.

Evidence against

  • The base layer collapsed: transactions -85%, new accounts -96% in a year.
  • Holders are still diluted: 81% of new supply is offset by nothing.
  • The yield is thin: captured revenue is 0.47% of a $4.8B market cap a year; the market pays 215× revenue.
  • The captured NEAR is held, not burned: it could be spent. The thesis depends on it not being spent.

Full argument, with every figure tied to the metric that produced it (a test fails if they disagree): THESIS.md.
