What no single team saw46 → 1

46 teams publish NEAR Intents (NEAR's cross-chain swap and settlement product. Users trade, NEAR takes a fee.) figures, and all 46 read them from the same source. We added an independent on-chain check. It agrees.

The investment thesis13–19%

of NEAR's new supply (NEAR creates new tokens every epoch to pay validators (about 2.5% a year). Each new token dilutes existing holders.) is offset by captured revenue (The part of Intents fees that NEAR keeps. It arrives as NEAR in three public wallets.). Owning NEAR is a bet that this revenue grows about 5.4×, not a claim that the token is cheap.

How it works

  1. Collect367 numbers from 59 public repositories, each with its exact sentence and the commit that wrote it.
  2. DownloadRaw data from DefiLlama, NearBlocks and a NEAR archival node, saved byte for byte with SHA-256 hashes.
  3. RecomputeEvery number for the day it was published, under each standard definition (30, 90 or 365 days…).
  4. Build and testA thesis from what replicates. Its 5 kill conditions run as code and are re-checked every day.

Re-checked every day. Three findings

What comparing 59 teams shows that no single thesis can

Confirmed independently

1The teams agree because they read one source

All 46 teams that publish NEAR Intents volume, fees or revenue cite DefiLlama, which reads datasets NEAR itself uploads to Dune. Their agreement is one measurement read 46 times, not 46 confirmations.

So we measured revenue a second way. It arrives as NEAR in three public wallets, and an archival node can read their balances at past blocks. Their growth is 0.98–1.01× the reported revenue over 30, 90 and 360 days.

The revenue figure behind every Intents thesis is real, and it can be checked on chain without trusting anyone.

Explained

2Where the numbers differ, the definition differs, not the data

5 teams publish NEAR's price change over a year, from +19% to +97%. 3 of them are within 5% of ours once the window and the end day match, the other 2 within 15%. Comparing two single days gives +41%; comparing two 30-day averages gives +91%, because the price jumped in early November 2025.

The same holds for base-layer fees in dollars (-55%, -40% or -28% in a year): each number is reproduced within 5% once the window and the end day match.

A single number without its definition means little. The spread between teams is the honest error bar.

Unexplained

3One figure does not replicate, and we say so

3 teams print 30-day Intents fees of $5,458,382 (or a rounded version), all committed the same day. Our total for the day before is $5.96M, 9% higher. No 30-day window in the chart comes within 5%: the closest is $5.78M, for the window ending 8 Nov.

Meanwhile 9 other teams' fee totals land within 5% of our line on their own publication days. Nor did the source rewrite that window: across our 3 daily snapshots its total moved by 0.01%.

We publish what we cannot explain instead of tuning it away.

The investment thesis

NEAR is a growth bet on Intents revenue, not a bargain

NEAR creates about 2.5% new tokens a year. Revenue from NEAR Intents now offsets part of that dilution, and the base layer offsets almost none. The thesis holds as long as that revenue keeps growing and stays in the wallets.

Evidence for

  • Demand moved to Intents and grows: $32.9B settled in a year; the 30-day average is +48% on a year ago.
  • NEAR keeps a rising share of fees: 18% over a year, 30% over the last 30 days.
  • The revenue is verifiable: the wallets hold 4.28M NEAR and their total has never fallen.
  • The offset is rising: 11.3% of new supply in the first half of the year, 14.6% in the second.

Evidence against

  • The base layer collapsed: transactions -85%, new accounts -96% in a year.
  • Holders are still diluted: 80% of new supply is offset by nothing.
  • The yield is thin: captured revenue is 0.48% of a $4.7B market cap a year; the market pays 210× revenue.
  • The captured NEAR is held, not burned: it could be spent. The thesis depends on it not being spent.

Full argument, with every figure tied to the metric that produced it (a test fails if they disagree): THESIS.md.
